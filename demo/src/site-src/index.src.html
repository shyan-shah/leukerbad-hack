The exercises

One station. Many treatments.

The same arm helps a weak arm after a stroke, loosens a stiff shoulder and strengthens a knee after surgery. It switches with the patient's plan.

Forward reach1 / 6

The patient holds the handle and reaches forward. The arm carries as much of the weight as they need, and gives less as they get stronger.

Measures reach in centimetres

The arm leads the hand round a smooth circle, keeping the shoulder moving freely in every direction.

Measures range of movement

Lifting the arm as high as is comfortable brings back the range of the shoulder, step by step.

Measures lifting angle in degrees

With the elbow at the side, the forearm turns out and back. It loosens a frozen or stiff shoulder.

Measures rotation in degrees

The arm rests against the shin and pushes back. It rebuilds the thigh muscle after a knee or hip replacement.

Measures force in newtons

The arm holds still and measures how hard the patient can push. The plan follows their measured progress.

Measures peak force

Safety

The therapist sets every limit. The robot never decides the treatment.

Running · forward reach, rep 7 of 12

One red button stops it. Try it.

Staff or the patient press it and the arm stops at once, and holds still where it is.

Arm speed0.12 m/s

Press the button to see what happens.

Limits set in advance

Every exercise has fixed limits on force, speed and range, set by the patient's specialist.

No expert needed on site

Any trained staff member can keep an eye on the station.

Every repetition recorded

The arm's own sensors record reach, angle and force.

What Vita does not do

  • Train walking, balance or fine hand function
  • Treat pain or the injury itself
  • Work with unhealed fractures or fresh surgical repairs
  • Train patients with acute inflammation or severe pain
  • Train patients who cannot say stop

When in doubt, the patient's specialist decides.
