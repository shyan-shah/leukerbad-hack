The problem

Rehab is costly, and demand is outgrowing the therapists.

1.7 billionpeople live with muscle, bone or joint conditions. The biggest reason people need rehab.
1 in 6people will be 60 or older by 2030. Ageing drives the need.
72%of US physical therapists say they are at or over capacity.

How rehab is done today

  • One therapist, one patient, one session at a time
  • $75–150 per session without insurance (US)
  • About 32 arm movements per stroke session. Recovery needs hundreds.

Why it gets worse

  • A projected shortage of 11 million health workers by 2030
  • Dutch physiotherapists warn a third may quit within five years
  • Waiting lists of over a year at rehab centre Klimmendaal, where this work is fully manual

Sources: WHO · Cieza et al., Lancet 2020 · APTA 2024 · Lang et al. 2009 · KNGF 2025.

The market

People who need to move again

  • After a strokeA weak arm that needs many repetitions
  • After a fracture or surgeryA stiff shoulder or elbow
  • After a knee or hip replacementA thigh muscle that has to regain strength
  • Older adults losing muscleStrength to stand up and stay independent
  • Parkinson’s and multiple sclerosisLarger, steadier movements

Close to home: gyms, care homes, pools, community centres. Anywhere with staff on site.

The solution

Rehab continues close to home

  1. Discharge from hospital or rehab
  2. Enrol at a nearby gym, care home or centre
  3. Scan the station with the Vita app
  4. Train, with staff keeping watch

Progress is measured and reported to their physiotherapist or rehabilitation centre.

The product

One station, six exercises

    The robot guides, resists or measures. Every repetition is recorded.

    Exercises approved by Katja, rehabilitation specialist at Klimmendaal for over 10 years. Today this is done fully by hand, and her centre has a waiting list of over a year.

    Safety

    One red button stops it

    • ProgrammedEvery exercise is set in advance, with limits on force and speed
    • No expert neededAny staff member can keep an eye on the station
    • EmergencyThey press the red button and the arm stops at once

    Why a station

    More practice between visits, for less.

    Physiotherapy alone

    • $75–150 per session without insurance (US)
    • Practice mostly happens during visits, once or twice a week
    • Every extra hour of training needs a therapist

    With a Vita station

    • A session costs a fraction of that, as part of a monthly subscription
    • Patients train between visits, several times a week, so they progress faster
    • Therapists spend their time where it is needed most, which takes pressure off care

    Appendix · Limits

    What the station does not do

    It does not treat

    • Walking and balance
    • Fine hand and finger function
    • Pain or the injury itself

    It is not for

    • Unhealed fractures or fresh surgical repairs
    • Acute inflammation or severe pain
    • Patients who cannot say stop

    The therapist sets every limit. The robot never decides the treatment.
